Four things it refuses to do.

Every one of these is standard in paid “PC optimizers”. All four are placebo at best.

Clean your registry

Unused keys cost nothing. Deleting a live one breaks something weeks later.

“Free up” your RAM

Windows caches on purpose. Emptying it makes the next few minutes slower.

Disable services in bulk

The ones that matter break in ways you will never trace back.

Promise “30% faster”

A number with no method behind it is marketing. Be sceptical of any tool that shows one.

If you think you’ve been hacked

Before you wipe the whole machine.

Reinstalling Windows costs you a day. Not reinstalling, when something really is on there, costs more. Canary looks for the specific ways malware stays on a PC — so the decision is based on something.

And the honest limit

It can raise suspicion. It cannot prove a PC is clean — anything running at kernel level hides from it.

So findings mean something. An absence of findings does not. If you have real reason to think you were hacked, reinstall — and this scan should not talk you out of it.
